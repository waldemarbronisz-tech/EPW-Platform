# EPW OS — Ela Power Watch

**Version {version}**

An open automation and visualization system for electrical
installations.

## Where It Came From

Commercial industrial controllers kept getting pricier while
microcontrollers and electronics kept getting cheaper, until the gap
stopped being about technology and became just price. Then came what
AI made possible — suddenly one person could build what used to take a
team.

EPW grew out of both: the conviction that it could be done cheaper, and
curiosity about how far one person could take it alone.

## What Makes It Different

Commercial SCADA systems tend to be either bloated with features nobody
uses, or missing the one you actually need.

EPW is built around a different idea: you configure everything on
site, from the device's own screen, with no laptop and no engineering
software required. Measurement points, descriptions, settings, screens
— straight from the panel in the cabinet.

## Where the Name Comes From

ELA and ADA are the author's daughters' names. ELA01 is the input card,
ADA01 is the output card. The system's name, Ela Power Watch, comes
from the older of the two.

## Where the Look Comes From

The interface is deliberately kept in a 1990s aesthetic. Partly out of
nostalgia, partly out of practicality: those layouts were designed for
readability on weak screens, in harsh conditions. In a control cabinet,
in bright sun or in the dark, they hold up better than more than one
modern interface.

## License and Availability

EPW is, and will remain, free. If you're looking for a controller for
your own home installation — you've found the right one. Use it,
modify it, build your own solutions on top of it.

## The Project's Governing Principle

**The screen informs, the hardware protects.**

EPW OS is never required for a protection function to work. Protection
is provided by electrical protection devices and an independent
hardware safety path. The visualization system can go down, and the
installation stays safe.
